## Sub 1
$$\text{lcm}(m,n) = \frac{mn}{\gcd(m,n)}$$
$$\frac{\text{lcm}(m,n)}{\gcd(m, n)} = \frac{mn}{\gcd^2(m,n)}$$
We construct potential function $\Phi_1(a) = \prod{a_i}$, $\Phi_2(a)=\sum{[a_i = 1]}$
There are 2 options:
1. $\gcd(m,n)=1$, $\Phi_2$ increases by $1$.
2. otherwise, $\Phi_1$ divides by $\gcd(m,n)$.
Because only one of $m,n$ decreases, there will be only one number greater than $1$ left.

## Sub 2

Construct:
$$(e_1^{(k)},e_2^{(k)},e_3^{(k)},\dots) \leftrightarrow x^{(k)}=\prod{p_i^{e_i}}$$
For $m=x^{(i)}, n=x^{(j)}$:
$$(e_k^{(i)}, e_k^{(j)}) \rightarrow (\min(e_k^{(i)}, e_k^{(j)}), \left|e_k^{(i)}-e_k^{(j)}\right|)$$
Notice that the gcd of $(e_k^{(i)}, e_k^{(j)})$ remains constant.
Therefore the final answer shall be:
$$\prod{p_i^{\gcd_k(e_i^{(k)})}}$$
